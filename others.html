Resources

Public-use microdata and practical starting points for empirical research.

United States Other high-income countries Low- and middle-income countries Cross-country and specialist sources AI & empirical research

Public-use does not always mean unrestricted access. Registration, research approval, or a licence may be required. These are browsing groups rather than strict country classifications; several collections span income groups.

United States

IPUMS USA ↗

Free registration

Harmonized census and American Community Survey records for population, education, migration, housing, and labor-market research.

IPUMS CPS ↗

Free registration

Monthly and supplemental Current Population Survey microdata for employment, earnings, income, and program participation.

ACS Public Use Microdata Sample ↗

Public download

Direct Census Bureau access to person and housing-unit records, with documentation and survey weights.

Panel Study of Income Dynamics (PSID) ↗

Free registration

Longitudinal family and individual data for studying income, employment, wealth, and intergenerational outcomes. Public-use files require registration; sensitive files are separate.

Survey of Consumer Finances ↗

Public download

Household balance sheets, assets, debt, and income. Public files include documentation for weights and multiple imputations.

Other high-income countries

UK Data Service ↗

Access varies

A gateway to UK surveys, including Understanding Society. Distinguishes open downloads, safeguarded files under an End User Licence, and controlled secure access.

European Social Survey ↗

Registration / terms

Repeated cross-country surveys of attitudes, trust, well-being, and social behavior. Coverage includes European countries at different income levels.

SHARE ↗

Free scientific-use registration

Survey of Health, Ageing and Retirement in Europe: longitudinal microdata for research on health, work, retirement, and family circumstances.

Statistics Canada public-use microdata ↗

Selected free downloads

Canadian household and individual microdata. Selected public-use files are free to download; the full collection and detailed data have other access arrangements.

Low- and middle-income countries

World Bank Microdata Library ↗

Access varies by study

A broad starting point for household and establishment surveys. Read the access policy on each study: catalogue listings do not always mean immediate downloads.

Living Standards Measurement Study (LSMS) ↗

Study-specific access

Household surveys on consumption, income, agriculture, and living standards, including longitudinal studies. Consult each survey’s documentation and access terms.

DHS / IPUMS DHS ↗

Free application / approval

Demographic and Health Surveys for fertility, maternal and child health, and household conditions. IPUMS provides harmonized extracts; DHS approval is required for the requested country samples.

UNICEF MICS / IPUMS MICS ↗

Research application / approval

Multiple Indicator Cluster Surveys on children, women, education, and household conditions. UNICEF authorizes access; existing permissions carry into IPUMS MICS.

Indonesia Family Life Survey (IFLS) ↗

Provider download instructions

Longitudinal household and community data from Indonesia, covering health, education, employment, and economic circumstances. Use the wave-specific data and documentation links.

World Bank Enterprise Surveys ↗

Data portal account

Firm-level surveys of the business environment, finance, infrastructure, and performance. Includes formal, informal, and micro-enterprise studies; coverage also extends to higher-income economies.

Cross-country and specialist sources

IPUMS International ↗

Free research application

Harmonized census microdata spanning countries at different income levels. A useful starting point for comparable person- and household-level analysis.

Association of Religion Data Archives (ARDA) ↗

Dataset-specific terms

Religion-related survey data and documentation. The archive also contains aggregate and contextual data; check the unit of observation before choosing a file.

AI & empirical research

Generative AI for Economic Research ↗

Anton Korinek’s Journal of Economic Literature guide provides examples for coding, data analysis, literature work, and other research tasks. Start with the article and follow its supplemental updates.

Published 2023 · AI Agents for Economic Research — August 2025 update ↗

A Practitioner’s Guide to Using Large Language Models and Generative AI in Economic History ↗

Andreas Ferrara’s practical guide, with worked examples and replication materials. Particularly relevant when constructing research data from historical sources; examples should be assessed for applicability to your own setting.

NBER Working Paper 35374 · 2026 · Full-text access may depend on your institution.

Using AI with research data

Check the dataset’s licence and AI-use rules before using any AI tool with it. Keep confidential or restricted records out of unapproved services. Validate generated code and results against the documentation, retain reproducible scripts, and record consequential AI-assisted steps.

Sources and access descriptions reviewed November 2026. Availability and terms may change; the provider’s documentation governs use.
